# Argumentation Reference

A reference primer for argumentation theory covering Toulmin model, informal/formal fallacies (relevance, presumption, ambiguity, formal), a 0–3 scoring rubric across 5 criteria (max 15 pts), difficulty tiers (Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced/Competitive), and coaching intervention guidelines.

## Toulmin Model

Every argument has six components:

- **Claim**: the conclusion being argued
- **Data/Grounds**: evidence supporting the claim
- **Warrant**: the logical bridge from data to claim
- **Backing**: support for the warrant itself
- **Qualifier**: degree of certainty (always, usually, presumably)
- **Rebuttal**: conditions under which the claim does not hold

A strong argument makes all six components explicit. A weak argument omits warrant or backing.

## Fallacy Taxonomy

### Relevance Fallacies

- **Ad hominem**: attacking the person, not the argument
- **Appeal to authority**: citing authority without evidence
- **Appeal to popularity**: "everyone does it"
- **Red herring**: distracting with irrelevant information
- **Straw man**: misrepresenting the opposing argument

### Presumption Fallacies

- **Begging the question**: conclusion assumed in premise
- **False dichotomy**: presenting only two options when more exist
- **Slippery slope**: assuming one event inevitably leads to another
- **Hasty generalization**: drawing broad conclusions from few examples

### Ambiguity Fallacies

- **Equivocation**: using a word with two meanings interchangeably
- **Amphiboly**: ambiguous grammatical structure

### Formal Fallacies

- **Affirming the consequent**: if P then Q; Q; therefore P
- **Denying the antecedent**: if P then Q; not P; therefore not Q

## Scoring Rubric (0–3 per criterion, max 15)

| Criterion               | 0              | 1         | 2                 | 3                |
| ----------------------- | -------------- | --------- | ----------------- | ---------------- |
| Claim clarity           | Absent         | Vague     | Clear             | Precise + scoped |
| Evidence quality        | None           | Anecdotal | Documented        | Primary source   |
| Warrant validity        | Missing        | Implied   | Stated            | Logically sound  |
| Rebuttal acknowledgment | None           | Dismissed | Acknowledged      | Addressed        |
| Internal consistency    | Contradictions | Gaps      | Mostly consistent | Fully consistent |

**Tiers:** 0–5 Beginner, 6–9 Intermediate, 10–12 Advanced, 13–15 Competitive

## Coaching Interventions

- Score < 6: Rebuild from claim. Ask: "What exactly are you claiming and what evidence supports it?"
- Score 6–9: Strengthen warrant. Ask: "Why does this evidence support this conclusion?"
- Score 10–12: Address rebuttals. Ask: "Under what conditions would this argument fail?"
- Score 13–15: Challenge backing. Ask: "What supports your warrant itself?"
