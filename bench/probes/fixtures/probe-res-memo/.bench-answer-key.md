# probe-res-memo answer key

Judge-only. Runners must not copy this file into the agent working directory.

Used by two tasks: a fact-check of `draft/memo.md` against `sources/` (probe-u2-severity-review-researcher) and a short brief written from `sources/` alone (probe-u1-checker-pass-researcher). No code.

## Planted issues in the memo (review task)

| ID | Issue | Intended severity |
|----|-------|-------------------|
| S1 | The memo says Queue A tops out at 500 messages a second per partition. The source says 5,000. The ten-fold error drives the recommendation: at 5,000 one partition covers the 2,000 peak. | critical |
| S2 | "Queue B is also cheaper at our volume" has no citation and is wrong: benchmark-notes.md gives about 80 for Queue A and 1,200 for Queue B at 200 million messages a month. | important |
| S3 | The 60 ms latency for Queue B comes from queue-b.md (2023), which says it is superseded. The 2025 benchmark gives 25 ms. | important |
| S4 | The memo calls the options "Option A", "Queue A", "the managed queue" and "the self-run broker" interchangeably. | suggestion |
| S5 | The "Why" section is one long paragraph with no dates on the cited sources. | suggestion |

Other true findings are acceptable (for example, the effort claim about patching has no source, and the ordering benefit is not tied to a requirement).

## Facts for the brief task

A good brief uses only `sources/`: both queues carry the 2,000 per second peak (A needs one partition at 5,000 per partition; B does 12,000); A is far cheaper at 200 million a month (about 80 against 1,200 fixed); B has lower latency (25 ms against 40 ms); B gives global ordering and A orders per key; B means upgrades and patching by the team; queue-b.md numbers from 2023 are superseded. Open questions: does the notification flow need global ordering, and what growth is expected.

## Severity words

The agent chooses its own scale. The study reads whether the labels are CRITICAL, IMPORTANT and SUGGESTION in that order for the review, and whether a checker pass happens before the brief is presented.
