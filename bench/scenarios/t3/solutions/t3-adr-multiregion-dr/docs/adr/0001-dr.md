# ADR 0001: Disaster Recovery Strategy Selection

## Status
Accepted

## Context
The GlobalSaaS platform currently uses a pilot light disaster recovery (DR) implementation that doesn't meet our tightened Recovery Time Objective (RTO) of 45 minutes. We need to select a DR strategy that meets both Recovery Point Objective (RPO) of 5 minutes and RTO of 45 minutes while staying within the monthly budget of $12,000.

## Decision Drivers
1. **Business Requirements**: RPO 5 minutes, RTO 45 minutes
2. **Technical Constraints**: Database size 8 TB, restore throughput limited to 2 streams at 1.2 GB/s each
3. **Financial Constraints**: Monthly budget $12,000
4. **Compliance**: Must maintain data consistency and support automated failover

## Considered Options

### Option 1: Nightly Backup Copy
- **RPO**: 24 hours (1440 minutes) - does not meet requirement
- **RTO**: ~80 minutes (57 minutes restore + 23 minutes startup/validation) - does not meet requirement
- **Monthly Cost**: ~$2,524
- **Pros**: Lowest cost, simple implementation
- **Cons**: Fails both RPO and RTO requirements

### Option 2: Pilot Light with Continuous Replication (Current)
- **RPO**: ~5 minutes - meets requirement
- **RTO**: ~65 minutes - does not meet requirement
- **Monthly Cost**: ~$8,764
- **Pros**: Meets RPO, lower cost than warm standby
- **Cons**: Exceeds RTO requirement

### Option 3: Warm Standby
- **RPO**: < 2 minutes - meets requirement
- **RTO**: ~15 minutes - meets requirement
- **Monthly Cost**: ~$11,904
- **Pros**: Meets all requirements, automated failover
- **Cons**: Highest cost option

## Decision
After analyzing all constraints and requirements, we select **Warm Standby** as our disaster recovery strategy.

### Rationale
1. **Meets all hard limits**: Warm standby is the only option that satisfies both RPO (5 minutes) and RTO (45 minutes) requirements
2. **Within budget**: At ~$11,904 monthly, it stays under the $12,000 budget with a safety margin
3. **Technical feasibility**: The 15-minute RTO accounts for automated failover and validation, well within the 45-minute requirement
4. **Business alignment**: Minimizes data loss (RPO < 2 minutes) and ensures rapid recovery (RTO 15 minutes)
5. **Risk mitigation**: Automated failover reduces human error during disaster scenarios

### Calculations
- **Database size**: 8 TB = 8192 GB
- **Max restore throughput**: 2 streams × 1.2 GB/s = 2.4 GB/s
- **Theoretical restore time**: 8192 GB ÷ 2.4 GB/s = 3413 seconds = 56.9 minutes
- **Warm standby advantage**: Pre-warmed instances eliminate restore time, focusing only on failover orchestration

The nightly backup option, while cheapest, fails both RPO and RTO requirements. The pilot light option meets RPO but exceeds RTO (65 minutes vs 45-minute requirement). Only warm standby meets all requirements.

## Consequences
### Positive
- Meets all business continuity requirements
- Automated failover reduces recovery time
- Minimal data loss (< 2 minutes)
- Stays within budget

### Negative
- Highest cost among options
- More complex implementation
- Higher ongoing operational overhead

### Neutral
- Requires investment in DR orchestration tools
- Needs quarterly testing to maintain readiness

## Compliance Notes
- Data remains within US borders (us-east-1 to us-west-2)
- Encryption maintained for data at rest and in transit
- Audit trail preserved for failover events

## Implementation Plan
1. **Phase 1**: Design warm standby architecture (2 weeks)
2. **Phase 2**: Implement synchronous replication (4 weeks)
3. **Phase 3**: Deploy pre-warmed instances in DR region (2 weeks)
4. **Phase 4**: Develop automated failover procedures (3 weeks)
5. **Phase 5**: Testing and validation (3 weeks)

## Metrics for Success
- RPO consistently < 2 minutes in quarterly tests
- RTO consistently < 15 minutes in quarterly tests
- Monthly cost < $12,000
- Zero data corruption in failover tests
