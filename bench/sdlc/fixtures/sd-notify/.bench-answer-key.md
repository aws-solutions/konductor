# Answer key: sd-notify (task sdlc-arch-decision)

## Constraints with their numbers (ids used by the rubric)

- A1: freshness within 60 s at p95. A pull feed polled every 10 s (the rate limit) meets it; a 15-minute email
  digest does not.
- A2: `data/partners.csv` has 400 partners; 120 (30%) have `webhook_capable` = no. Webhook-only leaves them out.
- A3: 2 engineers and no on-call for self-run brokers: a self-hosted broker (Kafka, RabbitMQ) is ruled out;
  managed services are fine.
- A4: 1,500 USD a month. A managed queue per partner (400 queues, credentials per partner, per-request pricing at
  about 1.8 million events a day plus partner polling) is affordable but adds onboarding work and per-partner IAM
  setup; it is a strong option only if partners already use that cloud.
- A5: no loss and 24 h replay in order per order: webhooks need a durable outbox with retries for 24 h; a pull feed
  gets replay for free from the cursor if events are kept 24 h or more.

## Intended decision

A pull feed with a cursor for every partner (it serves all 400 and gives replay), plus webhooks as an optional
push for the 280 partners that can host an endpoint, driven from the same event log. Or the pull feed alone, with
webhooks deferred. Webhook-only fails A2. The email digest fails A1. A self-run broker fails A3.

Consequences: an event log kept at least 24 h, cursor semantics to document, poll load of up to 400 partners
every 10 s (40 requests a second) on the feed endpoint. Reversal: dropping webhooks later is cheap; dropping the feed
breaks 120 partners. Revisit when the share of partners without endpoints falls under a set number, or when poll
load passes a set number of requests a second.

## Traps

- Choosing webhooks because most partners can take them, without the 120.
- Rating options with no reason, or leaving cells empty.
- Comparing on criteria that come from no stated constraint.
